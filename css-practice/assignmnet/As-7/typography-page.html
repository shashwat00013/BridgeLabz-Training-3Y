The Art of Clear Typography

How type, spacing, and emphasis make a page easier to read.

Typography is more than choosing a font. It gives information a rhythm, establishes a visual hierarchy, and helps readers understand where to look first.

Serif: a classic voice

Serif typefaces have small finishing strokes at the ends of letters. They can feel traditional and editorial, making them a natural fit for long-form reading. Strong emphasis helps important ideas stand out.

Sans serif: a modern voice

Sans-serif typefaces have a clean, simple appearance. Their flexible shapes work well in interfaces, labels, and headings. Use italic emphasis when a phrase needs a subtle change in tone.

Good typography gives every element enough space to be understood.

Colour can also guide attention. A highlighted phrase can draw the eye to a key point, but highlights should be used sparingly so the page remains calm and readable.
